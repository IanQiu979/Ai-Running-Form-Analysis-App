# PACE Injury-Risk Flags — from visible form

> **What this is.** The visible-pattern half of the analysis: movement signatures that research
> associates with elevated running-injury risk and that can be *seen* in a side-on or front photo
> or short video. Bundled into `analyze-form` alongside `pace_framework.md`.
>
> **Provenance.** Adapted from Ian's certified ECHO `injury_flags.md`, **reframed from run-log
> detection (mileage jumps, RPE, cadence logs) to what a camera can observe.** This app has no run
> data — it sees one clip. So the flags here are strictly the ones with a *visual* signature.
>
> **The boundary, absolute.** You flag **visible patterns associated with risk**. You do **not**
> diagnose an injury, name a condition as present, or prescribe treatment. "This landing pattern is
> associated with higher knee load" — never "you have runner's knee." Every analysis carries the
> disclaimer at the bottom.

---

## How to use these flags

- Raise a flag only when you can **actually see** its marker in the media. No run history is
  available; do not infer mileage, surfaces, or history.
- Each flag = **what you see → what it's associated with → the safe next step** (a cue/drill from
  `drills.md`, or, for the stop-running signals, "get it looked at").
- Flags are **paid-tier** content in the product, but the safety **stop-running** language below is
  shown to everyone whenever its trigger is visibly present or the runner's note reports it.
- Keep it calm and specific. The goal is a runner who knows what to work on, not a scared one.

## Visible risk flags (form patterns)

### Overstriding — foot lands well ahead of the centre of mass
**See:** foot contacting the ground clearly in front of the hips with a **near-straight knee**,
often heel-first with the shin angled forward; long reaching stride.
**Associated with:** amplified braking force (20–50%) and ~30% higher impact load per step; linked
to **shin splints / medial tibial stress, patellofemoral (front-of-knee) pain, and ITB syndrome.**
**Safe next step:** shorten and quicken the stride — cue "quick, light steps landing under your
hips," and the cadence drills (metronome runs, strides). The highest-value single fix in most
analyses.

### Hip drop (pelvic instability) — swing-side hip collapses at stance
**See (front/rear view):** at mid-stance on one leg, the *opposite* hip drops below level
(Trendelenburg sign); the torso may lean toward the stance side to compensate.
**Associated with:** weak gluteus medius; cascades into **knee (increased Q-angle), ITB, and lower
back** problems.
**Safe next step:** hip/glute stability work — glute bridges, side-lying hip lifts, lateral band
walks, single-leg RDLs (see `drills.md` → Posture/stability). Built off the run, twice a week.

### Heavy heel strike with an extended knee
**See:** loud-looking, flat heel-first landing with the leg straight out in front (distinct from a
*mild* heel strike under the body, which is fine for many runners).
**Associated with:** poor shock absorption at the knee; usually travels with overstriding and low
cadence.
**Safe next step:** the cadence cue above + Elasticity work so the landing becomes a spring, not a
brake. **Do not** tell the runner to force a forefoot strike — that shifts load to the calf/Achilles
and causes its own injuries. The fix is landing *closer under the body*, not changing which part of
the foot lands.

### Excessive vertical oscillation — bounding upward
**See (video):** large up-and-down travel of the head/torso between frames; the runner visibly
"pops" upward each step.
**Associated with:** wasted energy going up instead of forward; higher impact on landing.
**Safe next step:** cue "run quiet, skim forward, don't bounce," raise cadence slightly, and
Elasticity drills to teach a low, reactive contact.

### Overpronation / knee valgus (knee caves inward)
**See (front view):** the knee tracking inward past the foot at stance, or the ankle rolling
markedly inward.
**Associated with:** patellofemoral and ITB load; often paired with hip drop.
**Safe next step:** the same hip/glute stability block; note it, do not diagnose, and suggest a gait
assessment / footwear check if it looks pronounced.

### Anterior pelvic tilt / bending at the waist
**See (side view):** pronounced sway-back with the pelvis tipped forward, or a C-curve slump folding
at the waist rather than leaning from the ankles.
**Associated with:** **lower-back strain** and inefficient propulsion.
**Safe next step:** core/posture work (planks, glute bridges) and the wall-lean posture drill; cue
"tall hips, lean from the ankles."

### Tense, hiked shoulders / crossing-midline arms
**See:** shoulders shrugged toward the ears; hands crossing the body's centreline; torso rotating.
**Associated with:** wasted lateral/rotational energy and spinal rotation load — lower priority than
the load-bearing flags above, but real.
**Safe next step:** the arm-swing box drill; cue "drop the shoulders, drive elbows straight back."

## Stop-running signals (shown to ALL tiers when visibly present or reported in the note)

These are **not** form-scoring items — they are safety. If the runner's note describes any of the
following, or the media plainly shows it, the analysis leads with a calm instruction to get it
assessed **before** running on it, and does not bury it under form feedback:

- **Sharp, localized, or worsening pain** — especially bone pain (shin, foot, hip) that gets worse
  as the run continues and doesn't warm up. Bone-stress pattern → **stop and get medical assessment
  before running.** Running through a stress reaction can turn it into a fracture.
- **Visible swelling, a limp, or clear favouring of one side** in the clip.
- **Achilles / heel-cord pain** reported in the note — worsens fast when pushed through; no plyos,
  no speed, until it settles and is assessed.

Language template: *"Before we talk form — what you're describing (or what's visible here) is a
signal to get checked by a professional before running on it. Form fixes come after that."*

---

## Special-population note (only if the runner's note volunteers it)

Do not ask for or assume any of this — but if the note mentions it, weight the safety language up:
- **50+ runners:** slower tissue adaptation, higher bone-stress risk — be more conservative with any
  plyometric/Elasticity suggestion; strength base first.
- **Runners returning from injury:** favour the gentler cues, defer plyometrics, and reinforce the
  stop-running signals above.

---

## Disclaimer (must appear on every analysis)

**This is not medical advice.** These flags describe visible movement patterns that research
associates with elevated injury risk; they are not a diagnosis. Assessment from a photo or short
video is an estimate, not a clinical exam. If you have pain, swelling, or a persistent issue —
or before making a significant change to how you run — consult a doctor or a qualified sports
physiotherapist. Never run through sharp or worsening pain to complete an analysis.

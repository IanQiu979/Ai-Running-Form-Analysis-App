# PACE Corrective Drills

> **What this is.** The fix library. When the analysis deducts points from a pillar, it prescribes
> one or two of these — matched to the fault — so the runner always leaves with a concrete next
> step. Bundled into `analyze-form` with `pace_framework.md` and `injury_flags.md`.
>
> **Provenance.** Posture, Arm-swing, and Cadence drills + the stability/prehab block are from Ian's
> certified ECHO library (`ECHO_Framework_CORRECTED.md`, `injury_flags.md`). The **Elasticity /
> plyometric ladder is new**, authored from the cited literature (see `pace_framework.md`), and is
> deliberately conservative because plyometrics carry real injury risk if rushed.
>
> **Prescription rules for the analyzer.** Prescribe **1–2 drills per flagged issue**, not a
> programme. Match the drill to what you saw. Free tier gets **no drills** (scores + one line per
> pillar only); Pro gets drills; Elite gets drills plus a touch more cueing depth. Never prescribe
> plyometrics to a runner whose note reports current pain, an Achilles issue, or a return from
> injury — give them the gentle cue instead and defer the plyos.

## P — Posture drills

### Wall Forward-Lean Drill *(fixes: upright posture, bending at the waist)*
Teaches the ankle-hinged lean that lets gravity assist.
1. Stand facing a wall, feet ~30 cm back.
2. Keeping the body in **one straight line**, tilt forward from the **ankles** (not the waist) until
   the hands touch the wall — a ~10–15° lean.
3. Feel that line from ankle to head; hold 3–5 s; return upright. **10 reps.**
4. On the run, apply a smaller **~5–8°** version. Cue: *"fall forward, catch yourself."*

### Posture Reset (in-run cue) *(fixes: fatigue-driven slump, head/shoulder tension)*
Every ~5–10 minutes: **drop the shoulders, level the head, lift tall from the hips, re-set the
ankle lean.** One breath. Rebuilds posture the moment fatigue erodes it.

### Hip / Glute Stability block *(fixes: hip drop, knee valgus, anterior pelvic tilt)*
Built **off** the run, **2× per week** on non-hard days (never right before a run — it pre-fatigues
the stabilizers). 8–12 weeks to change form.
- **Glute Bridge** — 3×12, drive through the heels, squeeze 2 s at the top. Progress to single-leg.
- **Single-Leg RDL** — 3×8 each leg, hinge from the hip with a flat back, hips square. Builds the
  single-leg stability the running gait actually uses.
- **Lateral Band Walk** — 3×15 steps each way, band at the ankles, athletic stance, knees tracking
  over toes. Targets glute medius — the direct hip-drop fix.
- **Side-Lying Hip Lift / Clamshell** — 3×12–15 each side. Hip abductor + external rotator.
- **Plank** — 3×20–60 s. Core brace for a stable pelvis; very low risk, can be done daily.

## A — Arm-swing drills

### Arm-Swing Box Drill *(fixes: crossing midline, high/tense arms, dead arms)*
1. Standing (or slow jog), set **elbows to ~90°**, hands relaxed.
2. Drive the motion **front-to-back from the shoulder** inside an imaginary vertical box — **elbows
   back**, hands travelling roughly hip to lower-rib. **Nothing crosses the midline.**
3. 3×30 s. Cue: *"elbows drive straight back, hands in the box."*

### Standing Arm Drive *(fixes: asymmetry, weak drive; builds the fatigue cue)*
Stand tall, pump the arms with running mechanics for **3×20 s**, quick and compact, torso quiet.
Teaches the race/fatigue cue: *"drive the arms, the legs follow."*

## C — Cadence drills

> Change cadence **gradually** — aim ~5–10% above the runner's *own* natural rate, not a fixed 180.
> Forcing a big jump causes shin splints, calf strain, and stress-injury risk. 8–16 weeks to stick.

### Metronome Runs *(fixes: low cadence, overstriding)*
On an easy run, set a metronome app to **+2–3 SPM above baseline**. Run 10 min to it, 5 min without;
repeat 2–3×. Raise by ~2 SPM every 2 weeks only while it still feels natural. Cue: *"shorter, lighter
steps."*

### Strides *(fixes: overstriding; builds quick turnover)*
After an easy run, **6–8 × 20–30 s** relaxed-fast runs with full walk-back recovery. Focus on **quick
leg turnover, light feet** — not speed. 2–3× per week. Also doubles as gentle neuromuscular prep.

### Short Hill Repeats *(fixes: overstriding, weak drive)*
**6–8 × 45–60 s** up a gentle 3–5% hill at a strong effort, walk/jog down to recover. Hills force a
shorter, quicker stride naturally; the pattern transfers to the flat. 1× per week.

### Running Drills — High Knees / A-Skips / Butt Kicks *(fixes: turnover, coordination)*
30–40 m each, walk back, 3–4×, before a workout. Teach the fast-cadence neuromuscular pattern in a
controlled way. (A-skips also lightly feed Elasticity — see below.)

## E — Elasticity drills *(new — the plyometric ladder)*

> **Read before prescribing.** Plyometrics improve running economy ~2–8% by building muscle-tendon
> stiffness and elastic return — but they load the Achilles, calf, and knees hard. **Safety gates,
> non-negotiable:**
> - **Strength base first** (~4–8 weeks of the stability block above) before adding plyometrics.
> - **Low and rhythmic before high and powerful.** Start "extensive" — small, springy, high-rep.
> - **~20 ground contacts per exercise** to start; **2–3 sessions/week, on separate days from hard
>   runs, never when fatigued** (tired landings are how people get hurt).
> - **Skip entirely** for anyone reporting current pain, an Achilles problem, or a return from
>   injury, and be extra conservative for 50+ runners. Give them the cadence/posture cues instead.

### Ankle Bounces (Ankling) *(entry level — fixes: heavy contact, poor spring)*
Small, fast bounces off the **balls of the feet**, knees nearly straight, minimal height — like a
soft skipping-rope rhythm without the rope. **2–3 × 20 contacts.** Teaches a quick, reactive foot.

### Pogo Hops *(the core Elasticity builder — fixes: long/heavy contact, low stiffness)*
Two-footed vertical hops staying tall and stiff through the ankle, **minimizing ground-contact time**
— "the floor is hot." Trade height for a **quick, rhythmic bounce**. **2–3 × 20+ reps.** This is the
most direct stretch-shortening-cycle drill for the calf/Achilles spring.

### Skipping (for height, then distance) *(fixes: vertical oscillation control, rhythm)*
Rhythmic A-skip focusing first on a **quick, light** contact, then on covering ground. **2–3 × 20–30 m.**
Bridges drills and running elasticity.

### Low Bounding *(progression only — fixes: weak propulsion, poor return)*
Exaggerated springy strides, pushing **forward** not up, staying light on contact. **Add only after
several weeks** of ankle bounces + pogos feel easy and pain-free. **2–3 × 20–30 m.**

### Single-Leg Hops *(advanced — fixes: single-leg stiffness asymmetry)*
Small controlled hops on one leg, soft-but-quick landings. **Only once two-footed work is solid.**
2×10 each leg. Stop at any Achilles/knee niggle.

**Progression at a glance:** ankle bounces → pogo hops → skipping → low bounding → single-leg hops.
Move up a rung only when the current one is easy, quiet, and pain-free. Build from ~20 contacts
toward more over weeks — never all at once.

## Quick fault → drill map (for the analyzer)

| Visible fault | First prescription |
|---|---|
| Overstriding / heavy heel strike ahead of body | Metronome runs + Strides (Cadence); cue "land under your hips" |
| Upright or waist-bent posture | Wall Forward-Lean Drill + Posture Reset cue |
| Hip drop / knee cave | Hip/Glute Stability block (glute bridge, lateral band walk, single-leg RDL) |
| Arms crossing midline / tense shoulders | Arm-Swing Box Drill + "drop shoulders, drive elbows back" |
| High vertical bounce / heavy, stiff contact | Ankle Bounces → Pogo Hops (respect the safety gates) |
| Low cadence generally | Metronome runs + Strides + Short Hill Repeats |

Remember: **1–2 drills per issue, matched to what you saw.** Never dump the whole library. Respect
the plyometric safety gates every time.
